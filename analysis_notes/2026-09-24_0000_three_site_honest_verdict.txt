TWO SEPARATE THINGS ARE HAPPENING, NOT ONE CLEAN PATTERN:

1. WEATHER-ONLY CAN CATASTROPHICALLY FAIL ACROSS SCALE, AND LEAKAGE FIXES
   THAT TOO (reinforces Pillar 2, actually a SHARPER version of it):
   A/B (large utility-scale, 230-350kWp) -> C (small rooftop, ~55kW peak)
   in weather-only breaks completely (deeply negative R2) because the
   model has no way to know the target's scale is ~5-10x smaller - it was
   only ever shown weather -> big-plant-scale power. Adding current fixes
   this almost completely (R2 jumps to 0.95-0.96) because current itself
   scales with the specific array's electrical rating - it carries scale
   information that raw weather alone cannot. This is a CLEARER, more
   mechanistic version of the original Pillar 2 finding, not a weaker one
   - now we know WHY leakage helps cross-site: it's not just "more
   physically direct," it also anchors the output SCALE.

2. TRANSFER FROM A SMALL/NOISIER SITE TO A BIG ONE DOES NOT FULLY RECOVER,
   EVEN WITH LEAKAGE (a genuine complication, state it honestly):
   C -> A and C -> B stay weak-to-mediocre even with current included
   (R2 = -0.61 and 0.39) - nowhere near the 0.95-0.98 seen going the other
   direction. Plausible reasons (not proven, just plausible): (a) Plant C
   is structurally different - a single inverter within a 60-station
   shared-weather-station campus, not a dedicated plant with its own
   weather station, so its IRR correlation with power is weaker to begin
   with (0.86 vs A/B's ~0.99) - noisier training signal to learn from;
   (b) training on the SMALLEST, narrowest-range site and testing on much
   bigger/wider-range sites is a harder extrapolation problem in general,
   independent of leakage.

HONEST BOTTOM LINE:
- The MECHANISM (leakage transfers because it's physically direct AND
  scale-informative) is well supported, arguably better supported now
  than with 2 sites, because the 3rd site's weather-only catastrophic
  failure makes the "why" much clearer.
- The CLAIM "leakage gap is always bigger cross-site than in-site" does
  NOT hold cleanly in all 6 cross-site pairs - direction and site quality/
  comparability matter. Two of six cross-site pairs (C->A, C->B) stay weak
  even with leakage.
- THIS IS NOT A FAILURE OF THE FINDING - it's a more precise, more
  defensible version of it. A guide will trust "works well when going from
  well-instrumented sites to others, degrades when the source site is
  noisier/smaller/structurally different, and leakage's specific benefit
  is that it carries both physics AND scale" far more than an oversold
  "leakage always helps cross-site, no exceptions" claim.

DO NOT present the 3-site result as either "fully confirmed" or "broke the
theory." Present it as: refined and sharpened the mechanism, added an
honest boundary condition (source-site quality/comparability matters).
